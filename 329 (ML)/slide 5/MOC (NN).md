# Deep Learning: Fundamental Theory and Architecture

This document serves as the primary entry point for the study of [[Deep Learning]]. Deep Learning is a subfield of machine learning characterized by the use of hierarchical compositions of non-linear transformations to model complex data distributions.

Before proceeding, it is assumed the reader is familiar with [[Supervised Learning Foundations]], specifically the limitations of linear models in high-dimensional, non-linearly separable spaces.

## Core Conceptual Modules

1. **The Building Blocks**
   - [[The Artificial Neuron]]: The fundamental computational unit.
   - [[Non-Linear Activation Functions]]: The mechanism for introducing non-linearity.
   - [[Multilayer Perceptron Architecture]]: Stacking neurons into functional networks.
   - [[Neural Network Vectorization]]: Formalizing the linear algebra of forward propagation.

2. **Task Specifics and Evaluation**
   - [[Binary and Multi-class Classification]]: Mapping network outputs to probability spaces.

   - [[One-Hot Encoding]]: Representing categorical data for classification.
   - [[Cross-Entropy Loss Function]]: Defining the objective for optimization.

3. **Learning and Optimization**
   - [[Backpropagation and the Chain Rule]]: The mathematical derivation of gradients.
   - [[Optimization and Empirical Risk Minimization]]: The framework for parameter updates.
   - [[Stochastic Gradient Descent Variants]]: Practical algorithms for minimizing loss.
   - [[Advanced Optimization Algorithms]]: Momentum and adaptive learning rates.

4. **Theoretical Limits**
   - [[Universal Approximation Theorem]]: The mathematical justification for MLP expressivity.
