# Granular Noise

**Granular Noise** is a type of quantization error found in [[4.1 Delta Modulation System Overview|Delta Modulation (DM)]] systems. It is the counterpart to [[4.2 Slope Overload Distortion|Slope Overload Distortion]] and is most prominent when the analog input signal has a small or zero slope (i.e., it is relatively flat).

## Cause of Granular Noise

In a delta modulator, the staircase approximation $\hat{m}(t)$ is forced to change by a step of size $\pm\Delta$ at every sampling instant.

When the input signal $m(t)$ is nearly constant, the staircase output will continuously alternate between stepping up and stepping down as it tries to track the flat input. The quantizer output will toggle between `+1` and `-1`, causing the reconstructed signal to "hunt" or oscillate around the true signal value.

This oscillation creates a noise-like error component in the reconstructed signal. The difference between the flat input signal and the toggling staircase approximation is the granular noise. The magnitude of this noise is proportional to the step size, $\Delta$.

## Visual Representation

Imagine the analog signal as a flat, level path. The staircase approximation is forced to take a step up, overshooting the path. To correct this, it must take a step down at the next sample, now undershooting the path. This up-and-down sequence continues indefinitely as long as the signal remains flat, creating a granular, sawtooth-like error waveform.

## The Trade-off with Slope Overload

Granular noise and slope overload distortion represent the fundamental design trade-off in a simple delta modulator.

-   **To reduce granular noise**, one should choose a very small step size, $\Delta$. A smaller step means the oscillations around a flat signal will have a smaller amplitude, resulting in a cleaner output during quiet or slowly changing passages.
-   **However, a small $\Delta$ makes the system highly susceptible to slope overload distortion**, as it limits the maximum slope the modulator can track ($\Delta/T_s$).

Conversely:

-   **To reduce slope overload distortion**, one must use a large step size, $\Delta$.
-   **However, a large $\Delta$ will cause severe granular noise** when the signal becomes flat, as the staircase will overshoot and undershoot the signal by a large amount.

## Minimizing Noise

For a given input signal and sampling rate, there exists an **optimum step size** that provides the best compromise between the two types of noise, resulting in the minimum total quantization noise power.

This inherent trade-off in fixed-step-size delta modulation demonstrates its limitations. The ideal solution is to have a step size that can change according to the signal's characteristics: large when the slope is steep and small when the slope is gentle. This is the principle behind **[[4.4 Adaptive Delta Modulation|Adaptive Delta Modulation (ADM)]]**.
