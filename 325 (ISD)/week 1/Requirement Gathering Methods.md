

### 1. Observation

Watching users to identify pain points and behaviors.

- **Participant Observation:** Researcher joins the community/activity.
    
    - _Pros:_ Deep empathy, "insider" view, insights where questioning fails.
        
    - _Cons:_ Bias (loss of objectivity), ethical issues, very time-consuming.
        
- **Non-Participant Observation:** Researcher watches from outside.
    
    - _Pros:_ More objective, easier to observe body language.
        
    - _Cons:_ Missed context, distance reduces accuracy.
        
- **The Hawthorne Effect (Observer Effect):**
    
    - People change behavior when they know they are being watched.
        
    - _Mitigation:_ Build rapport, normalize observation, use multiple methods.
        

### 2. Interviews

- **Formats:**
    
    - **Unstructured:** "Go with the flow." Exploratory. Good when you know little.
        
    - **Semi-Structured:** Guide with themes, but allows detours. Best balance.
        
    - **Structured:** Strict script. No deviation. Requires high domain knowledge.
        
- **Stages:**
    
    1. **Preparation:** Research participant, draft protocol, schedule.
        
    2. **Execution:** Introduce project, dig for stories ("Tell me about a time..."), notice non-verbal cues.
        
- **Common Mistakes:**
    
    - **Leading Questions:** "Why do you love X?" (Assumes they love it).
        
    - **Compound Questions:** "How do you use X and who helps you?" (Two questions in one).
        
    - **Binary Questions:** "Do you like X?" (Yes/No answers kill dialogue).
        
- **Tips:** Embrace silence (let them think), use prompts ("Tell me more"), avoid correcting inconsistencies.
    

### 3. Focus Groups

- **Setup:** 6-9 people, 1-2 hours, moderator-led.
    
- **Purpose:** Clarify mental models, shared vocabulary, generate ideas.
    
- **Risks:**
    
    - **Groupthink:** People agreeing just to fit in.
        
    - **Dominance:** One loud person overpowers the quiet ones.
        
    - **Privacy:** Participants may not share sensitive info in a group.
        

### 4. Contextual Inquiry (CI)

Applied design ethnography. Combines observation + interviewing in the user's environment.

- **The 4 Principles:**
    
    1. **Context:** Go where the work happens (e.g., inside the kitchen, not a lab).
        
    2. **Partnership:** **Master/Apprentice Model**. User is the master; you are the apprentice learning their trade.
        
    3. **Interpretation:** Validate your observations with the user immediately ("I saw you do X, is that because Y?").
        
    4. **Focus:** Have a research goal, but stay open to surprises.
        

### 5. [[Surveys & Questionnaires]]

- **Best For:** Reaching many people cheaply, quantitative data.
    
- **Response Rate Strategies:**
    
    - Increase Rewards (Tangible gifts, sharing results, altruism).
        
    - Establish Trust (University branding, anonymity guarantees).
        
    - Reduce Cost (Short, mobile-friendly, convenient).
        
- **Question Types:**
    
    - **Open-ended:** Rich text, hard to analyze.
        
    - **Closed (Nominal):** Categories (Red, Blue, Green).
        
    - **Closed (Ordinal):** Ranked (Strongly Agree -> Strongly Disagree).
        
- **Design Tips:**
    
    - No acronyms.
        
    - No double negatives.
        
    - Pilot test with friends first to catch confusing wording.
