

### Definition

- A system that collects, stores, processes, and distributes data.
    
- **Goal:** Provide valuable information to users.
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - Banking apps & ATM systems.
        
    - E-commerce platforms (Amazon, Daraz).
        
    - University registration portals (USIS/BiS).
        
    - Supply Chain Management (SCM).
        
    - Hospital Information Systems.
        
    - CRM tools (Salesforce, HubSpot).
        

### 5 Core Components

1. **People:**
    
    - Individuals who interact with, maintain, or benefit from the system.
        
    - _Examples:_ End users, operators, management, IT support.
        
2. **Processes:**
    
    - Rules, workflows, and procedures defining _how_ tasks are done.
        
    - _Includes:_ SOPs, Business rules (e.g., "Verify payment before dispatch"), Policies.
        
3. **Technology:**
    
    - _Hardware:_ Servers, devices, sensors.
        
    - _Software:_ OS, Databases, Applications.
        
    - _Networks:_ Cloud infrastructure, Wi-Fi, Mobile data.
        
4. **Data:**
    
    - The raw material transformed by the system.
        
    - _Types:_ Stored (History/Logs), Real-time input (Sensors), Output (Reports/Dashboards).
        
5. **Organizational Context:**
    
    - The environment where the system operates.
        
    - _Factors:_ Business goals, budget, culture, legal/regulatory constraints.
        

### Design Philosophy & Usability

- **The "Door Quiz" Concept:**
    
    - **Norman Doors:** Doors that confuse users (Push vs. Pull) are examples of bad design.
        
    - _Lesson:_ If a user needs a sign to know how to use it, the design is flawed.
        
    - **Impact:** Bad design causes frustration, errors, and safety hazards (e.g., Aviation interface errors, Tide pods looking like candy).
        
- **Principles of Good Design:**
    
    - **User-Centered:** Built for humans, not just for function.
        
    - **Contextual:** Considers environment and social factors.
        
    - **Empirical:** Based on data and scientific method, not guesses.
        
    - **Inclusive:** Recognizes individual differences (age, ability).
        
- **What Good Design is NOT:**
    
    - NOT just applying checklists.
        
    - NOT using yourself as the model user ("You are not the user").
        
    - NOT just common sense (requires specialist knowledge).
