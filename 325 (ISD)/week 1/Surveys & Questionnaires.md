### 5. Surveys & Questionnaires

- **Definition:** A structured set of questions designed to collect data from a large group of stakeholders.
    
- **Best For:**
    
    - **Scale:** Reaching hundreds or thousands of people quickly and cheaply.
        
    - **Quantitative Data:** Collecting measurable statistics (e.g., "75% of users rate this feature as 'Critical'").
        
    - **Validation:** Confirming insights found in smaller, deeper studies (like interviews).
        

#### Response Rate Strategies (How to get people to answer)

You have to actively manage how people perceive the survey's value vs. its effort.

1. **Increase Perceived Rewards:**
    
    - **Tangible Gifts:** Offer a small incentive (e.g., $5 gift card, entry into a raffle).
        
    - **Altruism:** Clearly explain the purpose and how the results will benefit them or their community ("Help us design a better study space for _you_").
        
2. **Establish Trust:**
    
    - **Branding:** Use official university or company letterhead/branding.
        
    - **Anonymity:** Guarantee and emphasize confidentiality and anonymity, especially for sensitive topics.
        
3. **Reduce Perceived Cost (Time/Effort):**
    
    - **Keep it Short:** Only ask absolutely essential questions.
        
    - **Convenience:** Make it mobile-friendly and easy to complete on any device.
        

#### Question Types

The type of question dictates the data you get.

1. **Open-Ended:**
    
    - **Structure:** Allows the user to type anything (a text box).
        
    - **Data Type:** **Qualitative** (rich text, opinions, stories).
        
    - **Con:** Hard to analyze/process statistically.
        
2. **Closed Questions (Fixed Choices):**
    
    - **Nominal Scale:** Categories with no inherent order.
        
        - _Example:_ "Which operating system do you use most?" (Options: Windows, macOS, Linux, Other).
            
    - **Ordinal Scale:** Categories with a clear, ranked order.
        
        - _Example:_ "Rate your satisfaction with the system." (Options: Strongly Disagree, Disagree, Neutral, Agree, Strongly Agree).
            

#### Design Tips (Avoiding Bias)

The way you write the question is more important than the question itself.

- **Avoid Acronyms:** Use "Systems Development Life Cycle" not "SDLC," unless the audience is guaranteed to be experts.
    
- **One Question at a Time:** Avoid compound questions (don't ask about speed _and_ security in one sentence).
    
- **Avoid Double Negatives:** These confuse the brain and invalidate the data.
    
    - _Bad Example:_ "Do you not believe that unauthorized users should be unable to access the system?" (Too confusing).
        
- **Pilot Test:** Always run the survey on a small test group (friends, colleagues) first to catch confusing wording, typos, or design biases _before_ sending it to the main audience.
