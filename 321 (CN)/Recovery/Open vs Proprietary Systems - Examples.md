# Open vs Proprietary Systems - Real Examples

## **Example 1: WiFi (Open System)**

**Scenario:** You buy a new laptop

Your **Dell laptop** has a WiFi chip from **Intel**.  
Your home has a **TP-Link router**.  
Your friend visits with an **Apple iPhone**.

**What happens?**  
✅ All three devices connect to your router perfectly  
✅ They all communicate using the **IEEE 802.11** standard (WiFi protocol)  
✅ Because it's an **open system**, Intel, Dell, TP-Link, and Apple all built their hardware following the same publicly available specification

**Why this works:**
- IEEE published the WiFi standard openly
- Any company can read it and build compatible devices
- You're not locked into one brand

---

## **Example 2: Apple's Lightning Cable (Proprietary)**

**Scenario:** You want to charge your iPhone

Your **iPhone** uses Apple's **Lightning port**.  
You need a Lightning cable.

**What happens?**  
❌ Only Apple-approved manufacturers can make Lightning cables  
❌ Apple controls the specifications and licensing  
❌ You can't use standard USB-C or micro-USB (until recently)  
❌ If you want accessories, you must buy from Apple's ecosystem

**Why this is proprietary:**
- Apple keeps the technical details secret
- They require licensing fees (MFi program)
- Creates vendor lock-in - you must buy Apple accessories

---

## **Example 3: Email Protocols (Open System)**

**TCP/IP and Email:**  
You can send an email from **Gmail** (Google) → to **Outlook** (Microsoft) → read on **Yahoo Mail**. 

All use the same open protocols:
- **SMTP** (Simple Mail Transfer Protocol) for sending
- **IMAP/POP3** for receiving

Because these are open standards, email services from different companies work together seamlessly.

---

## **Example 4: Old Messaging Apps (Proprietary)**

**BBM (BlackBerry Messenger):**  
- Only worked between BlackBerry phones
- If your friend didn't have a BlackBerry, you couldn't message them
- When BlackBerry declined, users lost their entire messaging network
- No way to connect with other platforms

**Modern comparison:**
- **iMessage** (Proprietary): Only works well between Apple devices
- **WhatsApp/Signal** (Open Protocol): Works across all platforms

---

## **Real-World Impact Comparison**

| Aspect | Open System | Proprietary System |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Compatibility** | Works with any manufacturer | Limited to specific vendors |
| **Choice** | Multiple options, competitive pricing | Few choices, higher prices |
| **Innovation** | Anyone can build on it | Controlled by one company |
| **Longevity** | Survives even if one company fails | Dies if the company stops support |
| **Example** | USB-C, Ethernet, HTTP | Lightning cable, old printer cables |

---

## **Why Open Systems Won the Internet**

The internet is built entirely on **open standards**:
- **TCP/IP**: Connects all networks
- **HTTP/HTTPS**: Powers the web
- **DNS**: Translates domain names
- **Email protocols**: Connects all email services

If the internet used proprietary systems:
- You'd need different internet providers for different websites
- Emails from Gmail wouldn't reach Outlook users
- Devices from different brands wouldn't connect

---

## **Summary**

**Open Systems = Freedom, Compatibility, Competition**  
- Public specifications
- Interoperability between vendors
- Lower costs due to competition
- Survives beyond any single company

**Proprietary Systems = Control, Lock-in, Limited Choice**  
- Secret specifications
- Vendor lock-in
- Higher costs
- Dependent on one company's success
